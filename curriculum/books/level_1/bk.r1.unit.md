[r1.unit.d00]
This book is about three cups of water.

[r1.unit.d01]
A cup is a small container used to hold liquid like water.

[r1.unit.d107]
A unit is a thing used to measure or count something.

[r1.unit.d108]
A unit is a thing that can be counted or measured.

Three cups of water sat on the table. The cups were made of glass and held clear liquid. A breeze blew through the room and made the curtains move. The water in the cups did not spill. The table was wooden and smooth. Each cup had a small crack near the bottom.

[r1.unit.x05]
A bird flew past the window and landed on the branch outside. The bird did not fly away. The air felt still, though the breeze remained. The crack in the cup seemed to grow longer. The bird turned its head, wings lifting. It did not sing, but its breath was steady.

He did not move, not even to blink. He was afraid to make a sound. The water rose a little, like it wanted to climb.

The third cup had a small crack near the bottom. The crack looked like a thin line drawn by a tired hand. The boy watched it drip onto the table. He felt the wood grow warm where the drop landed. He thought about the first cup, now empty. He thought about the second cup, still full.

[r1.unit.x09]
The first cup spilled water onto the floor. The water spread slowly across the wood. The floor felt cold where the liquid touched. The boy looked at the wet spot and felt sad. He thought about the second cup, still full. He wondered if the third cup would break. He wanted to fix the crack.

The table stayed empty, but the story was not finished. The boy felt the air, cool and still, like a secret. He wanted to fix the table, not just the cup. He reached for a piece of wood, thick and warm. He thought about the cup, safe in the basket.

The air grew thick, like a blanket over his head. He reached for the basket, but it was empty. A single cup sat on the floor, cracked and dry.
