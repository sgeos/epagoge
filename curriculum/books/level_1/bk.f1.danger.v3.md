[f1.danger.v3.d00]
Some things hurt you. You cannot always see which. You have to be told.

[f1.danger.v3.d01]
You must be careful when you do something that could cause harm.

[f1.danger.v3.d02]
A cut is a wound made by something sharp like a knife or glass.

[f1.danger.v3.d03]
Hot means having high heat, like a fire or a baked pan.

[f1.danger.v3.d04]
Ill means you are not well, not safe, and need care.

[f1.danger.v3.s01]
The boy felt hot and sick after playing near the fire. He could not stand up straight. He tried to cry but could not make a sound. The air felt thick and heavy. The fire had burned the wooden branch. He had touched the hot stove and felt sharp pain.

[f1.danger.v3.s02]
He had touched the hot stove and felt sharp pain. He felt the burn deep inside, sharp and real. The pain spread like fire through his arm. He could not move, not even to cry. The air was thick, heavy, hard to breathe. He tried to stand but his legs gave way.

[f1.danger.v3.s03]
The fire was a danger he did not see. He felt the heat even when he closed his eyes. The pain spread like a secret no one could say. The room felt full of weight, hard to breathe. The blanket was too hot, the floor too cold. He thought of the apple he had left on the table.

[f1.danger.v3.s04]
She cleaned the cut with water and a clean cloth. The cloth was clean, but the pain stayed sharp. She thought about the apple, now gone, left behind. The room felt colder now, though the fire was out. The blanket stayed too hot, the floor too hard. She sat still, afraid to move, afraid to breathe.

[f1.danger.v3.s05]
He learned that even small things can cause great harm. The blanket felt too hot, the floor too hard. He sat still, afraid to move, afraid to breathe. The room was cold, though the fire was out. He thought about the past, about what came before. He had once been able to run, to jump, to play.

[f1.danger.v3.s06]
He stayed inside, warm and safe, until he felt better. The air felt thick, like something heavy was above him. He thought about the past, about what came before. He had once been able to run, to jump, to play. Now he sat still, afraid to move, afraid to breathe.

[f1.danger.v3.s07]
He never forgot the danger of hot things. He had been afraid to move, afraid to cry, afraid to breathe. The heat had come from above, from the lamp, from the light that once seemed safe. He tried to call for help, but his voice was gone, lost in the noise.

[f1.danger.v3.s08]
The boy was ill, but now he is safe and well. He felt warm, not hot, and his breath came easy. The blanket was soft, like a cloud, and he could move his arms. He thought about the lamp, but it was gone now. He did not feel afraid, not even when he heard a sound outside.

[f1.danger.v3.x13]
He woke up the next morning with a warm blanket and a cup of water. The light came through the window, bright and clear. He felt safe, not afraid, not alone. His body was weak but able to move. The water was cool, like a stream after rain. He drank slowly, not wanting to spill.

[f1.danger.v3.x14]
His mother read him a story about a boy who learned to be careful around fire. He could not move, only feel the burn, worse than ice, worse than fear. His mother said he was safe now, but he still felt the heat in his skin. He did not want to touch anything again, not even a toy, not even a book.

[f1.danger.v3.x15]
He promised to always ask before touching anything hot or sharp. He knew he had to stay safe, not just for himself, but for others too. He would ask before touching anything sharp or hot, even if it seemed small. He remembered the burn, the way it felt like fire inside his skin.
