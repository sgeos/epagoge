[m1.count.x_standing_for.s01]
Everything shown on a map has a meaning, and each number means something real, not just a guess.

[m1.count.x_standing_for.s02]
One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten — each number comes in order, no one skips, nothing is missing.

[m1.count.x_standing_for.s03]
The map shows a path, and each step means something real, not just a game.

[m1.count.x_standing_for.s04]
The child counted each thing, one by one, and knew that each thing meant something more than just a number.

[m1.count.x_standing_for.s05]
The opposite of one is not zero, but the idea of nothing, which is not a number at all.

[m1.count.x_standing_for.s06]
No one can count zero, because it means nothing, and nothing cannot be shown.

[m1.count.x_standing_for.s07]
Even when tired, the child kept going, because each number must come in order, and nothing can be skipped.

[m1.count.x_standing_for.s08]
Everything shown on the map has a meaning, and each thing must count, single, or zero.

[m1.count.x_standing_for.s09]
One apple sits on the table, and it is the first thing, so it means one.

[m1.count.x_standing_for.s10]
Two birds fly above, and they are the next, so they mean two.

[m1.count.x_standing_for.s11]
Three flowers grow in the garden, and they represent three, not anything else.

[m1.count.x_standing_for.s12]
The map shows the order, and no thing is out of place, not even one thing opposite.

[m1.count.x_standing_for.s13]
The meaning is clear: one, two, three, and so on, always in order.

[m1.count.x_standing_for.s14]
To count is to know what comes next, and nothing can be zero if it should be one.

[m1.count.x_standing_for.s15]
The single thing must be first, and everything else follows, never less or more.
