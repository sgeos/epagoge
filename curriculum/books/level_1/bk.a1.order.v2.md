[a1.order.v2.d00]
Open the box first. Then take out the cup. Not the other way.

[a1.order.v2.d01]
Order means the way things come one after another, like steps in a plan.

[a1.order.v2.s01]
The box sat on the table, waiting to be opened. He could not say why, but something deep inside told him to wait. The table felt cold, though the sun shone above. He looked around, but no one else was there. He thought of his sister, gone to bed early.

[a1.order.v2.x03]
The cup was blue and smooth, sitting inside the box like a secret. The cup felt warm, though it was made of blue glass. It looked like a secret held inside a box. He reached out, not sure what he was doing. The cup did not move, yet it seemed to call to him.

[a1.order.v2.x04]
She reached in and pulled it out, careful not to drop it. She held the cup steady, its blue glass cool against her palms. She wondered what it meant to hold something so small yet so full of weight. She set it down, not sure if she should keep it or let it go.

[a1.order.v2.x05]
The cup sat on the table, waiting for something to fill it. The blue glass caught the light, bright and clear. She thought about what it might hold, not just liquid, but meaning. Something inside her wanted to know, yet feared the answer. She reached out, not to take, but to feel the weight again.

[a1.order.v2.x06]
A small apple rolled out from the box, round and red. The air felt thick, like something was waiting to be said. She reached out, not to take, but to feel its shape. It felt heavier than it looked, full of secrets. She thought about the box, how it had been closed for years.

[a1.order.v2.x07]
She picked it up, wondering if it was meant to be eaten. She thought about the box, how it had been closed for years, and now something had come out. She could not explain why, but she felt it was not meant to be eaten. She placed it back on the table, not because she wanted to, but because she had to.

[a1.order.v2.x08]
The table was clean, but the floor had a small crack. The crack ran from the wall to the edge of the floor. She reached in, careful not to cut her finger. She pulled it out, wondering what it might say. There was only a line drawn across the back.

[a1.order.v2.x09]
She placed the apple beside the cup, as if they belonged together. The air felt thick, like a secret was about to be told. A line ran across the back, just like before. A sound came from the other side of the wall—soft, like a whisper. She wanted to run, but her feet stayed planted.

[a1.order.v2.x10]
Outside, the wind blew a leaf across the floor. She reached out, but stopped, afraid to touch it. She remembered the apple, the cup, the line on the back. Something inside her wanted to open the box. But the wind had brought more than a leaf—it brought a sound. A soft sound, like a whisper from behind the wall.

[a1.order.v2.x11]
She stood up and looked out the window. The sky was bright, and the sun was high. A bird flew across the sky, wings spread wide. She felt a pull, like the wind had lifted her. No one else was there, no one to ask. She had to know what was inside.

[a1.order.v2.x12]
The sky was bright, and the sun was high. She stood still, her breath slow, her heart loud. It felt warm, like it had been alive all along. She lifted it, the weight just right, not too heavy. She pressed her ear to the side—no sound, only air. She thought of the bird, flying above the trees.

[a1.order.v2.x13]
She closed the box, put it under the bed, and left the room. She thought of the bird, flying above the trees. The box was not empty, though it felt light. She could not explain why she felt afraid. She remembered the cup, warm and waiting. She reached out, not to take, but to feel.

[a1.order.v2.x14]
The cup sat inside the box, warm and waiting. It was not just any cup, but one that felt alive. She wondered if the bird had come back, or if it was only a dream. The air above the box seemed thick, like a curtain drawn tight. She reached out, not to take, but to feel the shape of what might be.

[a1.order.v2.x15]
The cup was inside the box, warm and waiting. She could not explain why, but she knew it was real. She reached out, not to take, but to feel the shape of what might be. The box did not open, but it seemed to breathe. She thought of her brother, gone far away.
