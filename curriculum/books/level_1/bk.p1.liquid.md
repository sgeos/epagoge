[p1.liquid.d00]
Water takes the shape of the cup. The empty cup is full of air.

[p1.liquid.d01]
Blow means to push air out of the mouth with force.

[p1.liquid.d02]
Breath is the air that comes out when you blow.

[p1.liquid.d03]
A bubble is a round ball of air inside liquid.

[p1.liquid.d04]
Drank means to take liquid into the mouth and swallow it.

[p1.liquid.d05]
Drip is a small drop of liquid falling slowly.

[p1.liquid.s01]
The cup was empty, full of air. Air can move, but not shape. Water cannot stay in one place. The cup holds water, not air. Water fills the space below. It does not rise above the rim. It stays low, not high. Water cannot climb out. It does not float above.

[p1.liquid.x07]
The bubble floated above the cup. It rose because air is lighter than water. The bubble stayed above, not below. It could not stay in the cup. It was not made to be held. The bubble was alive, but not real. It moved with the breeze. The breeze came from outside.

[p1.liquid.x08]
The cup sat on the table, still empty. Water could not be seen, but it was there. The shape of the cup made water stay inside. It could not leave, even if it tried. The cup held water as if it belonged. No other shape could do this. It had no name, no voice.

[p1.liquid.x09]
A boy picked up the cup and held it close. He felt the shape of the cup, warm from his hand. Water inside moved, not like a fish, but like a thought. The cup held water, and water shaped itself to the cup. It could not be more or less than the cup allowed.

[p1.liquid.x10]
He blew into the cup, and air rushed out again. He wondered if air could be shaped too. No one had ever tried to hold it. He reached to touch it, but it slipped away. The air was not a thing to keep. It belonged to no one, not even him.

[p1.liquid.x11]
The cup felt warm in his hands. He thought about how the water stayed, unlike air. The cup held it, not letting it drift away. He could not see the shape of water, but he felt it. It was alive, moving inside the cup. He wondered if the cup knew it was holding something.

[p1.liquid.x12]
The bubble floated above the cup, bright and round. It seemed to know the air above was its home. It moved through the room, past the window, into the light. The cup did not mind. It had held water, not air. The bubble had no shape of its own. It only followed the breeze, soft and slow.

[p1.liquid.x13]
The cup tipped, and the water dripped onto the floor. The water flowed across the floor, thin and bright. The cup had been tipped, and the water was gone. The floor felt cold, and the water moved slowly. The shape of the water changed with the floor. It took the shape of the floor, wide and thin.

[p1.liquid.x14]
The boy picked up the cup and carried it to the sink. The boy poured water from the cup into the sink. The water flowed down, bright and thin, across the drain. The cup was empty, but still held air, waiting. The boy felt the cool water touch his hand.

[p1.liquid.x15]
The cup still held air, waiting to be filled again. The boy looked at the empty cup, thinking about what came next. Air inside the cup could not be seen, but it was alive and real. The cup could hold more than water—like light, or sound, or time. It could be shaped by a hand, or fall from a shelf.
