This book is about why some things remain while others go, and how we argue about what we see.

A child sits near the lake, watching the sky, and wonders why the balloon is gone and the apple remains. The sky feels heavy, like it might crack open. The apple stays, red and round, still alive in the grass. The balloon floats above the trees, bright and drifting. No one saw it go, but it must have left.

The balloon appears above the trees, bright and floating, but then it hides in the clouds. The balloon hides above the clouds, but the apple stays alive in the grass. The child feels alone, yet not afraid, because the sky is still bright. No one sees the balloon go, but the air feels different.

The air feels thick, but not heavy, like a breath held too long. They believe the story must stay, not go, not hide, not be lost.
