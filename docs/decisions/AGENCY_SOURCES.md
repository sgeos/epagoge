# Agency texts as lexicon candidates

**Recorded 2026-10-10.** Operator direction. Material through grade six is
a level-two scan candidate. Entering eighth grade is level three, which is
the row already in `LEVEL_CALIBRATION.md`, and the operator confirmed that
reading on this date. A work that continues past grade six is split. Its
younger band is a level-two candidate. Its older band is a level-three
candidate.

A word that is too advanced for level two is not dropped for that reason.
It is scheduled at the lowest level that may hold it. The concept must
already be taught at or before that level. Levels three to seven accept
such a word. They still have no schedule, and the gate does not require
one. The dictionary file for a level is created when the first definition
at that level is written. See `WORD_FORMS.md` for the admission path.

## What may be scanned

**United States federal works by federal employees are not under copyright
in the United States.** That is the basis already recorded in
`READING_LEVEL_RESEARCH.md`. Text written by a partner, a contractor, or
anyone else, and an image that carries its own copyright notice, is not a
candidate even when it sits on the same page. A frequency count is a
proposal. Nothing from the page is copied into the corpus. What can enter
the repository is this project's own definition of a word.

Dale-Chall and the New General Service List stay unused. Their licensing
hold is unchanged.

### Level three, entering eighth grade

The older band of each split work in the table above. Also the NASA
magazine for ages 9 to 14, which is the largest unread NASA prose set in
this list, and the grades 7 and 8 portion of the grades 5 to 8 climate
guide. GLOBE pages are often written by a partner. A GLOBE page is a
candidate only after that page is identified as a United States federal
work. The kindergarten to grade 5 bands are level two on that condition.
The grades 6 to 8 band is level three on that condition.

Foreign agency text is not treated as public domain. A later licence
reading can move a row from this table into the candidate table. Until
that reading, the row is identified and unused.

## What this record does not do

It does not assert that scanning these pages will reach the approximate
level-two target of about ten thousand headwords. A scan proposes words
that are frequent in a source. Each admission still needs one definition
the level can ground. Words already given a review decision stay decided,
including thee, thy, thine and unto, which remain deferred.

## First pass, measured 2026-10-10

Three sources were fetched and scanned at the same threshold of five.
NASA children's earth pages, a thin StarChild level 1 sample, and USGS
Water Science School pages. The scan found 469 words frequent in at least
one source. After the project filters the pool held 243. 151 headwords
were admitted at level two and 16 at level three, where the sense is past
level two and the concept is already taught. Distinct headwords through
level two rose from 2,159 to 2,310, measured. The other strings in the
pool were left without a review decision.

The StarChild level 1 fetch did not follow links out of the section pages,
so most of that band was not read. The grades 5 to 8 portion of the
climate guide was not split into its own file. Forest Service readers,
further NOAA pages, Energy Information Administration pages, Fish and
Wildlife booklets, Ben's Guide, and National Park Service booklets were
not fetched. This pass does not claim the approximate target.
