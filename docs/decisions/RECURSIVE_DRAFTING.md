# The corpus cannot be drafted in level order

**Recorded 2026-09-26, operator observation.** `THREE_PROBLEMS.md` treats
the levels as three problems solved in sequence, and that is right about
their shapes and wrong about their order.

**Level one needs a large backported corpus built on concepts introduced at
level two.** So level one is not finishable before level two is designed,
and the natural reading order is not the drafting order.

## Why level one needs level two to be designed first

**A picture book cannot revisit.** Sixteen spreads at fifty words is eight
hundred words, and the curriculum's claim is that a concept is met again at
increasing depth. A single level-one book introduces a concept and has no
room to return to it, so depth at level one comes from **more books about
the same concept**, not from longer ones.

**Which concepts deserve that treatment is a level-two question.** A
level-one book exists to prepare a reader for the level-two module that
develops the idea. Until the level-two schedule says which ideas those are,
a level-one author is guessing at what to prepare for.

**This is what the backport already did, on a small scale.** Three
level-two concepts were brought down into level-one picture books on
2026-09-25. `BACKPORT.md` records it as an experiment; this record says it
is the general case.

## What this means in practice

**"Level one is complete" can only ever mean complete for the current
generation of the level-two schedule.** The word band is closed and every
unit has a book, and both facts are relative to a schedule that will grow.

**The order of work is therefore: level-two lexicon, level-two schedule,
then level-one backport, then the level-two corpus.** The operator's
direction of 2026-09-26 that a complete draft lexicon must precede corpus
drafting is the first step of exactly this.

**It is recursive and not circular.** Level two is designed against the
concept graph, which exists. Level one is then written against that design.
Nothing waits on itself.

## The correction this forces on the framing

`THREE_PROBLEMS.md` says volume is a level-one concern and that coverage
and consistency bind at levels two to six. **The operator's correction is
that coverage and consistency bind at level one too.**

Volume was the level-one bottleneck as measured, and that measurement was
real. But it was the bottleneck **because the format limits what else can
be done**: eight hundred words cannot carry depth, so the only lever
available at level one was more of it. That is a property of the picture
book, not evidence that coverage does not matter there.

**Read together**: level one has the same coverage and consistency problem
as every other level, and a format that cannot solve it within a single
book. It solves it across books, which is why it needs to know what the
later levels will ask for.
