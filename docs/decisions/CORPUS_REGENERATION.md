# Corpus regeneration by trained models

**Decided 2026-09-24.** Two proposals, two different answers, and one
objection that settles both for the research phase.

## The proposals

**Downward.** A level N model regenerates the level N-1 corpus.

**Lateral.** A level N model regenerates its own level N corpus.

## The objection that settles both, for now

**Any regeneration by a model trained on the corpus makes the corpus
dependent on that training.**

The ablation compares a curriculum ordering against a random topological
ordering. If the corpus were regenerated by a model trained under one of
those orderings, the corpus would no longer be independent of the treatment,
and **the experiment would be circular.**

**During the research phase the corpus is frozen.** Regeneration is a
production-phase technique. It can also be run later as its own experiment,
with its own control, asking whether regeneration improves the curriculum.
That is a legitimate question and a separate one.

## Downward regeneration, evaluated on its merits

**The intuition is good.** A teacher who understands a subject deeply
explains it simply, and a level N model has seen what level N-1 was
preparing for. That is information the original generator did not have.

**The obvious implementation fails for a reason worth stating.** The level N
model in this project is **weaker than the teacher, not stronger.** The
teacher is a thirty-billion-parameter instruct model. A level-three model
here might be three hundred million parameters on a few billion tokens. It
writes worse prose than the teacher does, and it does not understand level N
better than the teacher does. It understands *the curriculum's* level N.

So the analogy inverts. This is not an expert simplifying for a novice. It
is a novice rewriting an expert's work.

**The sound form separates the two roles.** What the level N model uniquely
has is knowledge of **what it actually learned**, which is measurable by
data attribution over the level N-1 records. That signal does not require
the student to write anything.

> **The student measures. The teacher regenerates.**

Attribution identifies which level N-1 records helped and which did not.
The teacher then produces more of what helped. Quality of generation is
preserved and the student's information is captured.

**Accumulate, never replace.** Verified 2026-09-24, the replace paradigm,
in which each generation discards prior data and trains only on new
synthetic examples, is the dangerous default, and even a small fraction of
synthetic contamination causes collapse. The accumulate paradigm, keeping
prior data and adding on top, is the survivable one.

## Lateral regeneration, evaluated on its merits

**Harder to justify.** A model regenerating its own level introduces no new
information. Its output can only narrow toward its own distribution, which
is the classic collapse setup.

**It is not hopeless, because this project has a verifier.** Verified
2026-09-24, a reliable verifier is among the most effective mitigations, and
self-verification has been shown sufficient to prevent collapse even in
fully synthetic regimes with finite-sample error bounds. With source
grounding, executable checks, and the sampled audit already specified,
lateral regeneration becomes rejection sampling rather than naive
self-training.

**Two costs remain even so.**

Coverage narrows toward what the model already does well, which is the
opposite of what a curriculum wants.

**Provenance breaks.** The corpus would no longer attribute to verified
teacher generation, and auditable provenance is the project's strongest
differentiator. Trading it for a quality gain that is not demonstrated is a
poor bargain.

## Summary

| Proposal | Research phase | Production phase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Downward, student rewrites | No. Circular, and the student is weaker | No. Use the sound form instead |
| Downward, student measures and teacher regenerates | No. Circular | **Yes, accumulating rather than replacing** |
| Lateral | No. Circular | Only with the verifier, and at a provenance cost |
