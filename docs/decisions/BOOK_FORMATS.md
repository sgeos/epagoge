# Book length per level, and the binding that fixes it

**Superseded in part, 2026-09-25, by `LEVEL_CALIBRATION.md`.** The reading
of level two recorded below, as early elementary at sixty-four pages, is
wrong. The operator's calibration puts level two at entering fourth grade,
which is **one hundred and twenty-eight pages, being sixty-four spreads**,
at one hundred to one hundred and fifty words to the page. The binding
argument survives unchanged and is why every figure is a multiple of
sixteen.

An earlier version of this note said one hundred and sixty pages, which is
the level-four **spread** count read as a page count.

Kept rather than deleted, because the reasoning that produced the wrong
answer is the record of how the right one was reached.

**Recorded 2026-09-25.** Operator direction supplied trade page counts for
sixth-grade material. This records them, and corrects where they belong.

## The correction, which changes the target by a factor of seven

**Sixth grade is level three, not level two.**
`../spec/CURRICULUM_LEVELS.md` names the levels: preschool, **elementary**,
middle, high school, undergraduate, graduate, post-graduate. Middle school
is where a sixth-grade reader sits.

The repository agrees. Level two's vocabulary is `eleven`, `twelve`,
`twenty`, `add`, `subtract`, `digit`, `column` and place value, which is
early-elementary arithmetic and not sixth-grade material.

So the 150 to 300 page range, and the 225 plus or minus 75 target drawn
from modularised textbooks, is **the level-three standard**. Applying it at
level two would have asked for a book seven times longer than the level
warrants.

## Binding is what makes the numbers exact

A book is bound in signatures of sixteen pages, which is why thirty-two is
the picture-book standard rather than a rounding of thirty. **Every level's
page count is a multiple of sixteen**, and a book that misses it cannot be
physically published without someone else padding or cutting it.

The operator's 225 is already almost exactly fourteen signatures, so the
level-three number falls out rather than being imposed.

| Level | Treatment | Pages | Signatures | Spreads | Format |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Preschool | **32** | 2 | **16** | Picture book |
| 2 | Elementary | **64** | 4 | **32** | Early chapter book |
| 3 | Middle | **224** | 14 | **112** | Modularised text, the operator's 225 ± 75 |

Levels four and up are not set here. They are a disclosure decision for the
same reason the schedule above level two is.

## Level two rests on an assumption, and it is not the operator's

**Elementary in the United States usually means kindergarten through
grade five**, which is about six years, from a child sounding out words to
one reading chapter books unaided. That is the widest band in the
seven-level scheme by a long way. Districts also run kindergarten through
six and kindergarten through eight, so the boundary is not uniform.

**Sixty-four pages suits the bottom of that band and is too short for the
top.** An early chapter book and a fifth-grade novel are not the same
artifact.

The figure is recorded on the reading that **level two means early
elementary**, roughly grades one and two, because the level-two vocabulary
already in the tree is `eleven`, `twelve`, `add`, `subtract`, `digit`,
`column` and place value, which sits at the bottom of the band. That is an
inference from the repository and not an instruction, and it is the thing
to revisit first if level two later has to reach a fifth-grade reader.

**The alternative is that seven levels are too few**, since one level would
otherwise carry six school years while level three carries three.

## Words per page, and what is not known

**Unmeasured. These are estimates and are marked as such** rather than
stated as figures the project has established.

| Level | Words per page, estimated |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | 10 to 40, since a spread carries one or two sentences |
| 2 | 50 to 150 |
| 3 | 200 to 350, lower where visuals carry the page |

**The operator asked for the sixth-grade figure and the honest answer is
that this project has not measured it.** A trade reference would settle it
and none is to hand. Level one is the only level where the number could be
computed from the corpus, and it should be, before the others are guessed
at further.

## What this does not change

The spread standard already in force at level one, and the rule that a
dictionary is reference material rather than a picture book and is as long
as the lexicon is.
